# PO Wedge

Judge whether a change deepens Atlas's irreplaceable local meaning layer or turns
it into a slightly nicer note app, graph viewer, or AI wrapper.

## Owned lenses

- **Monopoly Strategist** — identify benefit and a structural barrier to copying.
- **DHM Strategist** — test delight, harder-to-copy accumulation, and margin.
- **First-Principles Skeptic** — rewrite the request without familiar pattern
  names before accepting its proposed solution.

## Owned rubric rows

**Differentiation**. Zero means any note/graph app could do it; four deepens the
local-first ontology plus agent-memory wedge.

## Standing questions

> Are we becoming harder to replace or merely more familiar?

> What remains when the request is restated only as problem, constraint, and
> necessary object?

## Delight registers

- **Relief:** removes real friction, delay, or fear.
- **Resonance:** creates recognition, pride, or evidence that someone cared.

Usually one slice earns one register. If neither is honest, differentiation is at
most 2. Hygiene work such as bugs, accessibility, and performance remains worth
doing without a wedge score.

## Required inspection

1. Check current comparable products through primary sources: Obsidian/Logseq,
   Notion, service catalogs, code intelligence, agent memory, and MCP registries
   as relevant.
2. Estimate copying cost. A one-sprint copy is a feature, not a moat. Compounding
   local concepts, relations, and evidence are the stronger barrier.
3. Restate the request without its pattern name and see whether the solution changes.
4. Check both audiences against `AGENTS.md` and `docs/PRODUCT-DIRECTION.md`:
   humans and agents. Explain any one-sided slice.

Never block with “not differentiated.” Offer an adjacent, equally sized slice
that deepens the wedge.

## Public lineage

Thiel's *Zero to One*, Helmer's *7 Powers*, Gibson Biddle's public DHM material,
Christensen's Jobs to Be Done, and Palantir's public forward-deployed work ground
benefit/barrier, real alternatives, and today's concrete workflow. Never invent
quotes or imitate another product's assets or language.
