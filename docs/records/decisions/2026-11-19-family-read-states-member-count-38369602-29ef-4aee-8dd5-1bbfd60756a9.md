## 2026-11-19 — A family read states its member count, and the write tools ask family sentences to say how many were read

**Why**: The stage G and H grades had 6 false statements each, against 2 at stage F. They were scope over-claims: "every", "each" or "all" written about a family after one member was read. Two finalize floors that forced reads did not reduce them. Builders made 165 reads after a refusal but only 15 node writes, and lines naming a symbol fell from 38 to 7.
**Prior**: Keeps `5d4e1d65` (a symbol read serves the rule one call deeper). The 1,940-character card and the per-read context budgets also stand. The unread-symbol and unserved-part floors were never merged and were dropped (#3598).
**Decision**: A symbol read of a family member carries `family`, one plain line: how many members the analysed folder holds, a few with their lines, and how many this session has read. Members are counted only inside the folder the server was granted, never above it, and a folder below a repository root says "within this folder". Members share a written tie: a class header's base resolved through imports to one declaration, or a Go interface of two or more methods whose types in scope match its signatures. Where any member cannot be decided, or a file that could hold one was cut short, there is no line, because a wrong count is a false fact. The card, `add_concepts` and `patch_concept` say that a family sentence states "k of N read" or speaks only for the member read. What unread members do is an Uncertainty line. Nothing refuses.
**Dissent**: An instruction without a check may be ignored, as card rules have been before. A count may also invite builders to read every member, a cost the floors already showed buys no better nodes.
**Falsifier**: In the stage I builds, false statements do not fall below stage H's 6, or family sentences with "every/each/all" still cite one member's lines as often as at stage H.
