## 2026-11-16 — The download headline names the change and the map, and the trust line speaks plainly
**Why**: The owner found the Korean headline ("the agent writes the code, / I read it as a map") weak: a Korean sentence does not open with the first-person "I", and it lacked impact. The owner also questioned the trust line ("signed and notarized by Apple · no Atlas backend"): industry terms a first-time visitor cannot weigh. The playground control was a ghost button that read as plain text.
**Prior**: Keeps 4c4690a9-666a-4aa5-a9ea-c346a61d4c29 (Moru, a plain claim, the app's window); changes only the claim's words and the trust line's wording. Amends decision (83) and 36af7b02-78cb-441f-9c92-ad20cf7db271 on the hero trust line as the page's signing claim, and undoes the 2026-10-15 demotion of the playground to a ghost button.
**Decision**: The headline is *AI changes the code. / See it on one map.* (Korean: "code an AI changed, / at a glance on the map"; ja and zh to match), sized per locale to fill the claim column. The trust line says what a visitor gets: free, no sign-up, checked by Apple for malware (notarization; not "no warning", since macOS still asks once on first open, and not App Store review); the Windows line keeps that the unsigned beta may raise SmartScreen. The absence of an Atlas backend is stated in the open-source section. The playground is an outlined *Try in the browser* button.
**Dissent**: "AI" is broader than "coding agent"; the lead underneath names Claude Code and Codex, so the headline trades precision for punch. The headline also promises a little more than the mechanism: Atlas marks the concepts to recheck after a commit and shows no code or authorship, which the lead states exactly. The trust line no longer answers on the first screen whether code leaves the machine.
**Falsifier**: A first-time visitor cannot say from the first screen that Atlas maps code an AI changed, or reads "checked by Apple" as a promise of no warning at all or as Apple's approval of the app.
