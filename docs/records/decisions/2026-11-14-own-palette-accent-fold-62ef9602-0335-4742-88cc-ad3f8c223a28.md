## 2026-11-14 — The app frame takes its own palette; the accent tone split folds back into one
**Why**: The owner judged the workbench "still unimpressive and AI-looking" and chose the app-wide frame as the first thing to redo. The surface, ink and indigo values were another product's published palette (near-black blue-grey, violet-indigo fills, violet alpha tints), which is the look a generated dark dashboard defaults to.
**Prior**: This overturns the 2026-08-03 decision "`tone: 'accent'` was a marker, not ink: split tone into two". Its standing contra-assertion fired: with neutral tints the accent ink is AA on every base and tint composite, so `accentOnTint`, its lint pairing rule and the pairing scans are removed, and `accent-ink-contrast` now holds both accent inks to AA on every listed ground.
**Decision**: Surfaces and ink become warm graphite neutrals (canvas `#0b0b0a`, panel `#121211`, elevated `#1b1b19`; ink `#f3f2ee` / `#d2d0c9` / `#a4a29b` / `#86847e`). The filled primary action is paper white with dark ink. The tint and line ladders become neutral alphas of that paper white, so selection and hover carry no hue. One calm blue (`#8fb0ea`) is the ink for links, focus and sparks. Kind colours, signal tones and the map's own `--map-*` tokens are unchanged. Token names keep the word `indigo`; only values move, so reverting is a value revert in `app/styles/tokens.css` and `app/styles/base-root-alpha.css`.
**Dissent**: A monochrome frame can read as another familiar house style and loses the product's one recognisable hue in chrome; indigo as protagonist was a deliberate identity.
**Falsifier**: If the owner finds the frame lifeless once cards, buttons and type follow, or if selection becomes hard to find without hue (a selected row confused with a hovered one in use), restore a hue to the tint ladder and re-open the tone split.
**Owner**: Project owner, 2026-11-14 choice of the app-wide frame; palette chosen and applied by Claude.
