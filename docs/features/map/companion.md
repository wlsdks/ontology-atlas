# Companion home, project growth and idle adventure

#### Companion home, project growth, and idle adventure

- The existing entries open a centered, fixed-height pixel world. The original fox walks, reads, organizes ideas, and sleeps in a workshop, with an expedition scene for battles. WASD/arrows and ground clicks move; E acts only on the nearest reachable camp object; distant objects show a small glint instead of duplicate key prompts. The camp portal opens adventure selection. I opens inventory, K skills, M the map, J the journal, N opens the creature field guide, B opens temporary expedition blessings, Space dodges the next attack, and Escape closes the inner panel before the game. A camp constellation visibly fills with recorded concepts, relations, implementation links, and wiki pages; E at its altar opens the growth ledger. The bottom menu uses labelled pixel item slots. Walking advances with distance traveled, and every pose shares a foot contact line with its ground shadow. Mouse controls and a coarse-pointer direction pad provide alternatives. Panels and long journal entries fit through paging instead of outer scrolling. The map toolbar and verified agent-work icon retain their separate ownership.
- Project records provide starting power and further growth on manifest refresh: 24 XP per unique valid-UID concept, 8 per resolved graph relation between those concepts, 16 per concept recording an implementation path, 40 per listed wiki page, and 4 per 40 body words (at most ten units per document). Wiki furniture and ambiguous concept UIDs are excluded. Category high-water marks avoid repeat-opening rewards and retain progress through cleanup. Code work counts when reflected in these records; source correctness and coding time are not measured. The project journal shows current counts, saved highs, and separate project, reading, and adventure balances.
- Explicitly previewing a unique concept earns 5 reading XP; one personal discovery or question earns 15. Editing a discovery does not award it again. Current concept links resolve by UID; missing concepts retain history without an invented live destination. Reading is an activity, not verified understanding.
- T opens camp learning expeditions: choose a current concept, read its authored responsibility and exclusions, compare one recorded direct dependency with its declaring document, identify a review candidate, and save a personal explanation or question. Missing dependencies remain unknown; recorded links do not establish semantic truth, runtime impact, or transitive effects. The in-game reader pages the complete saved body and original dependency fields without leaving the lesson. Source identity, modification time, and witnessed declarations are checked again before completion. Failed source reads and saves retain the draft. Reading and reflection reuse the existing 5/15 XP limits, with equal credit for uncertainty and corrections; there is no mastery score or canonical approval. Leaving combat for learning requires an explicit return to camp.
- A topic with a saved reflection is marked **My note** and opens the personal record directly, with its original recording date and learned/corrected/uncertain kind. Viewing the note triggers no source read or reward write. An open note remains readable when its current source disappears; re-exploration is unavailable until a current unique topic exists. Explicit re-exploration performs fresh source reads, retains the prior explanation, and reuses the existing XP limits. Unfinished edits take priority over stored text. Roomy folios show short paragraphs in full; narrow, enlarged-text, and multiline notes retain paging and their position across in-game source visits.
- Six fictional regions contain 36 destinations with distinct original environments, enemy rosters, guardians, difficulty, and route effects. Region IDs are game content, never domain UIDs. Project and reading XP unlock destinations and contribute to attack and level-earned skill points; combat XP alone cannot unlock them. Six skills increase attack, HP, potion healing, active-wave damage, gold, or adventure XP. Inventory has three upgradable equipment items, consumable potions, concept cards, guardian relics, and once-claimable supply chests every five victories.
- The first destination has a distinct direct-exploration sector, selected from the adventure map without starting the automatic combat save. A full-frame, camera-following pixel tile map accepts WASD/arrows, contextual E, Q scan, and the existing I/T/L panels; touch has its own direction pad. The fox walks on collidable floor tiles, meets Curio, and follows a harmless signal mote through three locations. A gate must be activated beside it after those encounters and a saved reflection on a current, uniquely resolved project concept; a past quest claim or saved gate position alone cannot open it. The exit marks a local sector clear without awarding ontology meaning or an extra combat reward. E at the completed exit returns to camp. Sector position, encounters, and clear are stored in a separate project-scoped record. This is one directly traversable pilot; the other 35 destinations still use the existing automatic expedition model. The pixel characters and tiles are original code-drawn shapes and do not reuse another game's art.
- Battles run automatically against 108 original species, including 18 guardians, in five floors of three rooms, with a guardian in the fifteenth room. Archive Grove's 18 dark-woodland creatures, Clockglass Foundry's 18 brass, glass, and steam creatures, and Inkwell Marsh's 18 ink, reed, and parchment creatures use distinct generated pixel silhouettes; the remaining 54 species retain their earlier original art. Foundry and Marsh combat sprites are grounded to their individual transparent bounds. A project-independent pixel foreground grounds both combatants while the existing destination art remains behind it; camera framing keeps them together at supported widths, and movement stays on the visible combat floor. The fox's attack, traveling rune, impact, enemy windup, and hero response follow the saved combat phases. A warning above the fox marks the Space dodge window; pressing Space in that window starts a short sidestep immediately. A small mark above a ready creature replaces the large floor target ring; clicking or tapping it triggers the same saved Q wave as the keyboard, and both action slots show remaining turns. Opening an in-game panel pauses turns and pose animation without spending the unfinished fraction of a turn; closing it resumes battle, while leaving the game still permits bounded offline catch-up. Reduced motion retains the warning, cooldown, and action without the sidestep travel. Each floor makes another three-way blessing choice available; eight blessing types can combine attack, HP, healing, wave damage, rewards, cooldown, and reflected damage. Offers are deterministic within a saved run; stale-run choices are rejected. Blessings reset between expeditions while permanent project growth, equipment, and skills remain. Space provides a cooldown-bound dodge, and Q fires a knowledge wave. Returning or redeparting during the final guardian’s loot display banks the clear reward exactly once. Auto retry is optional: when enabled it starts a fresh build after completion or recovery from defeat; when disabled the fox returns to camp. Highest floor and successful expeditions remain in the map journal. Gold buys equipment upgrades and potions; 1 consumes an owned potion. Six combat trait families provide armor, stronger attacks, regeneration, life drain, double strikes, or wave resistance. Six route conditions change rewards, post-victory healing, enemy health, or damage. The field guide records actual sightings and defeats, and maps retain separate completion counts. Older saves default to empty discovery and completion records; retired domain destinations return to camp with accumulated progress intact. Monsters do not diagnose code or ontology defects.
- During an expedition, M opens its five-floor itinerary. Choose a rest, supply, or elite path for the next floor; the current floor stays unchanged. Rest restores up to 25% max HP once on entry and reduces each victory by 2 gold; supplies adds 15% enemy HP and 3 gold per victory; elite adds 25% enemy HP, 15% damage, 2 gold, and 6 adventure XP per victory. The preference survives retries, while applied path history resets per run. The fixed progress control remains accessible while the world camera moves.
- L, the camp notice, and the inventory quest ledger open twelve once-claimable quests. Nine use current construction, wiki, reading, or reflection evidence; three optionally use completed ACP writes. Current counts, unique target identities, and a resolved relation source support claims rather than construction high-water marks. Learned, corrected, and uncertain reflections count equally. Each claim saves its identity and guardian relic reward together; historical rewards survive later correction or deletion, while missing current targets are labelled unavailable. Rewards do not accept ontology meaning.
- ACP quests require the same loaded native root, a fully correlated allowed/completed writer receipt, an eligible concept creation or body update, and a uniquely resolved current target. Pending, failed, rejected, legacy, foreign, ambiguous, missing-target, destructive, and approval-only records grant nothing. Repeated writes to one target count once; retained receipt history is bounded, so absent evidence is not proof that work never occurred. An assistance preview sends only the displayed request after an explicit action, through the existing agent workbench and approval process. With no ready receiver, it stays open and reports that nothing was sent.
- The sword, cloak, and spellbook have guaranteed enhancement without destruction. Forge limits are +5/+10/+15/+20 after zero/two/four/six quest claims, reachable through ordinary non-ACP activities. Enhancements below +5 cost gold; later tiers also consume one/two/three relics. Every five levels adds a personal equipment bonus: 2 attack, 5 max HP, or 0.25 knowledge-wave multiplier respectively. Current/next stats and exact costs precede the action; feedback follows successful persistence. Existing higher-level equipment is retained, with quests gating only further enhancement.
- Device-local game saves are scoped by one unique project UID; copies of the same project share progress. Away progress is capped at four hours, consumed once, and evaluated using previously saved power before newly observed construction rewards. Backward clocks grant nothing. Receipts follow successful persistence. Canonical ontology files and approval state are untouched.
- Closing the game unmounts its simulation and movement loops; hidden documents pause timers and catch up on return. A fully recovered camp does no periodic storage writes. Reduced motion updates immediately when the preference changes, suppressing sprite, ambient, and projectile animation while retaining user-directed movement and game state. Project scans run only in the open, lazy-loaded game when its manifest changes. Assets are original generated artwork, packaged as WebP with provenance recorded beside them. Illustrated folio pages, route vignettes, and an equipment forge retain semantic text and keyboard controls; artwork does not encode changing project facts.
- The original journal preserves up to 50 personal memories and keepsakes without conversion into XP. Failed writes retain drafts; unreadable saves remain intact for explicit reset. Personal memories, reading history, and fictional adventure resets remain separate. Adventure reset retains project construction high-water marks.
- Enjoyment, retention, and improved understanding remain unmeasured. Technical checks establish behavior and presentation, not those outcomes or semantic qualification.
