# Moru cast: tools, emotions, conversations, locomotion, wardrobe and life

The art work of 2026-11-22 on Moru, the mascot, as it stands after four rounds of fixes and three
independent judgings. Words used here: a **pose** is one frame of a clip; a **clip** is one strip of poses
that starts and ends on the neutral pose; a **cast** member is one Moru variant (a body palette plus a
costume); the **life runtime** is the code that decides what each Moru on a floor does next.

## Where it lives

The owner asked on 2026-11-22, translated: "Put everything made for Moru into the project itself, with no
separate backup; temporary files are cleared often and will disappear." So the generators are in the
repository and the media they make is not: Git keeps only media the product ships
(`committed-media.contract.test.ts`).

- Generators: `scripts/brand/moru-cast/`. Entry: `pnpm brand:cast-preview` (`preview.mjs`), which writes
  every clip, the measurements, the contact sheets, the cast line-up, the life prototype page and the farm page
  into `output/moru-cast/` (git-ignored; `MORU_CAST_OUT` moves it). It runs in about two minutes.
- Pose drawing: `rig.mjs` (cuts the neutral into parts), `engine.mjs` (draws a pose from parts, including
  the hand-typed raised flippers, the turned roll and the front turn), `art.mjs` (costumes, bubbles, icons,
  props, marks), `tools.mjs` (tool scenes), `palette.mjs`, `scene.mjs` (clips as data, the pace solver, the
  64px rebuild, GIF writing) and `clips.mjs` (every clip).
- Life runtime: `life/behaviour.mjs` (pure), `life/wardrobe.mjs`, `life/export-atlas.mjs` (sheets and anchor
  tracks), `life/page.js` (the Canvas 2D host), `life/build-page.mjs` (one self-contained page).
- Farm: `farm/` (on hold; see below).
- Measurements: `catalogue-pace.mjs`, `palette-check.mjs`, `palette-contrast.mjs`, `check-64-neutral.mjs`,
  `actor-gap.mjs`, `life/wardrobe-check.mjs`, `life/measure-life.mjs`, `life/pose-variety.mjs`, and the
  Playwright scripts in `life/browser/` (CPU, heap, per-tick cost, frame capture, cover check).

The life runtime's prototype stays beside the generators; the app's TypeScript port is in
`src/shared/ui/marks/moru/life/` (see "In the app: Work status"). The Work status rooms already use the cast palettes and head pieces through
`scripts/brand/build-moru-cast.mjs` (`pnpm brand:cast`), which this work builds on.

Every generator uses whole-pixel integer operations only. sharp decodes and encodes PNG and GIF and never
resamples; an enlargement repeats pixels in JS. `preview.mjs` reproduces the prototype folder byte for byte:
214 of 215 files compared on 2026-11-22 (94 strips, 94 GIFs, 16 sheets, four measurement files and the life
atlas, wardrobe sheet and wardrobe check). The life page differs only because four unused variables left its
inlined script.

### How a pose is made

The neutral is cut once into torso, tail, two flippers and face marks. The torso changes height or width
only by removing or repeating whole rows and columns identical to their neighbour, so the outline keeps its
thickness; everything else moves rigidly by whole pixels. Posing the rig at rest reproduces the catalogue
neutral with 0 differing pixels. A raised flipper is one of three hand-typed paddles (up, diagonal, side), a
pixel to a letter in the body's two-row stair rhythm. There is no arm: as in the shipped catalogue, a lifted
flipper stays a small lobe lying against the body, so the reach a pose asks for is shortened to at most 15px
from its seat for the flipper at the body's edge and 9px for the one on the belly, and two who clap lean in by
7px each for their flippers to meet. Each paddle is outlined
3px and cut at the floor row. The pace solver eases the body before the flippers, so a keyed reach is kept. The
roll turns the body in sixteen steps of 22.5 degrees, drawing the outline again at its own thickness, setting
the eyes and nose down whole and turning the whiskers and mouth by the body's own angle. On a turned pose a
small worn piece loses only detail a line or a dot wide and keeps a solid patch (the pink inside an ear); one
that stands out of the silhouette is seated 3px in; the wing is worn as one broad notched shape.

The 64px poses are made as the catalogue makes them: each pose is rebuilt on the catalogue's 256px work
grid (a moved piece of the neutral takes its 2x2 block from the work drawing; a new pixel is doubled) and
reduced with the catalogue's own `reduceMoru`. The work drawing comes from `workGridPoses('blink')` in
`scripts/brand/build-moru-sprites.mjs`.

## Cast and palettes

One body; a palette swaps only the body fill and the belly tone, so the outline, eyes, indigo flippers and
tail and the shading stay and every clip works with every variant. One accent is added: pink `#f2b3c6`.
Each tint keeps the original tone's luminance, so contrast holds by construction (body on the app canvas
13.1 to 13.2:1); the builder is the one deliberate exception (7% darker, 12.29:1).

| Moru | none | `#d6d3c9` / `#fff1d7` |

which agent or model is working.

## Wardrobe slots

`life/wardrobe.mjs`. Any piece goes with any palette; adding a piece is a few typed rows and an anchor.

- **Head (10):** horns, horn and mane, small horns, long ears, hard hat, beanie, sun hat, party hat, bow,
  flower crown.
- **Body (7):** work vest, scarf, cape, apron, neckerchief, small wing, back plates.
- **Accessory (5):** square glasses, satchel, badge, bell collar, backpack.

A piece is one to three sprites, each tied to an anchor (crown, face, chest or back) and a layer: behind the
body, on the skin (clipped to the body fill), or in front. Flippers, props, marks and bubbles are drawn last,
so no piece covers a flipper. Anchors are exported for every pose of every clip. Soft pieces lag their anchor
by 1 or 2px and trail while walking; ears and the wing move one in-between picture a pose toward the shape a
pose asks for. A swap tosses two head pieces through the midpoint between their wearers. In a clip the ears,
wing and mane are drawn where the head was a pose ago and the scarf's end where the back was two poses ago,
by 2px at most; nothing trails on the first and last pose, through a turn or on a turned body.

## Life runtime

`life/behaviour.mjs` is pure: no DOM, timer, clock or randomness of its own. Its interface is `createWorld`,
`addMoru`, `tick(world, dtMs)`, `due(world)`, `raise(world, id, state)` and `nudge(world, id, kind)`.

- **Behaviour choice.** Each Moru has curiosity, energy and sociability in 0..1 that drift with what it does.
  A free Moru weighs what is possible now (wander, go to a spot, greet, high-five, ask, swap hats, nap together,
  prank on tiptoe, nap alone, or a travelling move where there is room); moods set the weights, the last four
  actions weigh less and the last two partners are skipped. A partner agrees only if free and close. After
  playing together a pair often chains a second and third thing. An asked question is answered from a set,
  never with the last answer.
- **Steering.** Three lanes give depth. Walking is one stride at a time (9, 13, then 16px). Every stopping
  place must be a body's width (100px) from every other Moru and every destination, in any lane, falling back
  to 76px on a floor too full for it; a Moru standing in front of another steps aside first. Distances are
  taken between the middles of the silhouettes, tail included. A room's Moru lives in the front lane and never
  leaves it. Nobody walks past anybody, in any lane: a walk or a travelling move is taken only where nobody
  stands or is headed along the whole way, a walker stops a body's width short of whoever it meets and its
  walk ends there, and the only playmates are the two neighbours. A floor too full for that (twenty in
  1,920px) lets them pass. A
  spot is spoken for from the moment it is chosen, and a walk whose place beside a friend has been taken turns
  into a walk to the nearest free place. A travelling move
  starts only when its whole path is clear, and a walker waits for one crossing its path. A turn is five drawn
  poses through a front-facing one.
- **Real events first.** A Moru that stands for a room plays only clips with a near-neutral pose at least
  every 350 ms, so `raise` cuts in within 400 ms on the 10 Hz clock. While its state lasts it is claimed and
  takes part in nothing else. Play is shown only where it cannot be read as agent state.
- **Five-mover budget.** At most five Moru act at once, plus up to two short things on the spot for a Moru
  that has stood eight seconds; the rest blink, glance and sway. A real event skips the budget.
- **Frame cache.** One Canvas 2D canvas on one `setTimeout` clock at 8 Hz, slowed while nothing changes and
  stopped when hidden, off screen or under reduced motion. Composed poses are kept as `ImageBitmap`s, capped at
  24 MB, least recently shown out first, never one on screen; a blink redraws only the eyes.
- **Measured.** Playwright WebKit, 20 Moru at 1920x1080, five acting: renderer plus GPU 5.5 and 6.1 % CPU
  against 0.0 % standing still; cache hit rate 84 to 86 %; peak JS heap 3.8 MB in Chromium. Without a renderer
  (`life/measure-life.mjs`, 600 s, 8 Hz): 0 pops, 0 same-lane overlaps and nobody standing in front of a
  room's Moru with 8, 12 and 20 Moru, and 0 piles. Two bodies with
  their middles nearer than 80px, standing or passing, in any lanes: 0 of 4,800 ticks with 8, 12 and 14 Moru
  (3,468 with 12 before nobody walked past anybody), 23 with 16, every tick with 20. With 12 Moru 91 things
  were played together in the ten minutes (32 before) and 56 approaches were given up. On the built page
  (`life/browser/cover-page.mjs`, six casts of 180 s): 0 ticks with anybody drawn over a room's Moru.

## In the app: Work status

Shipped on 2026-11-23: the lobby. `pnpm brand:life` (`scripts/brand/build-moru-life.mjs`) draws the life clips the
lobby plays into `public/brand/moru-life-{base,over,wardrobe}.png` and `moru-life.json` (296 KB together; the two
sheet layers wrap at 1,024px). The runtime is `src/shared/ui/marks/moru/life/` (`behaviour.ts`, `presence.ts`,
`wardrobe.ts`, `lobby-canvas.ts`); the lobby is `MoruLobby` in `src/widgets/work-tracks/ui/`.

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Where | The building's ground floor, under the floors and above the shelf, named "Lobby" like a floor; its floor continues the building's, with a doorway at each edge |
| Fixtures | A bench Moru nap on, a mat they play on, a crate they peek round and a plant; drawn once a size on a canvas behind the Moru |
| Crew | 4 plus one per worktree no live session holds, at most 12 |
| On the floor at once | Usually 2 to 4, never more than 5, at 1,440, 1,512 and 1,920px alike |
| Coming and going | Through the doorway at an edge and out through one, one walk at a time, 4 to 10 s apart; an edge is used only while nobody stands within 260px of it |
| Spacing | Standing middles at least 150px apart (a body and a half); two playing together stand 92px apart and then walk off to a clear place |
| Moving at once | At most 2, plus 1 short thing on the spot |
| Looks | A newcomer takes a palette and a head piece nobody on the floor wears; body piece 60 %, accessory 40 % |
| Moves | Walking, the turn, `roll` and `tiptoe` (a prank); no bound, run or belly slide; no ladder or pond spot, so `climb` and `fish` are not shipped |
| Clock | One `setTimeout` at 8 Hz, up to four ticks' time while nothing changes; stopped while hidden or off screen; reduced motion holds a still lobby |
| Pose store | `ImageBitmap`s capped at 8 MB, least recently shown first, never one on screen |

The rooms' own clips from this cast, each for an observed state only:

| Observed in a room | Clip |
|---|---|
| Working | `desk-type`, then the tool clips in turn at loop ends (`hook-wrench`, `gear-start`, `magnifier-read`, `switch-flip`, `book-stack`, `timer-set`, `lock-check`, `sapling-tend`) |
| Two tools working | Every third loop of the one behind, together: `ask-idea`, `teach`, `ask-fish`, `idea-shrug-sulk`, redrawn where the pair stands (the front one turns to the other and back) |
| Quiet or no session | `waiting` (held neutral) |
| Untouched for long | `nod-off` |
| A lock left behind | `feel-worried` |
| A folder gone | `sigh` |
| A new commit | `crate-send`, then `feel-proud`; the one behind a pair plays `feel-happy` (`merge-high-five` needs three actors) |

Shipping these per character as the rooms' rows are would add about 3.8 MB. Instead `pnpm brand:room-clips`
ships one grey strip each (15 strips, 281 KB, plus a 25 KB description), and a room dresses a strip for its
Moru once, with the palette and head piece `pnpm brand:cast` uses, into an in-memory image. A clip starts
only on the neutral pose and only once it is dressed; until then the room keeps its row. Tool clips use a
192px cell and a talk a 248px cell, both drawn past Moru's 128px box without moving the room's layout; the
pair's two sprites show the same talk through windows 120px apart and wait on the neutral pose for each
other. Every strip opens and closes on the neutral pose (the generator fails otherwise), and the lobby's
per-frame trace keeps any body within 2px of its clip's own drawn move (`smoothness.test.ts`).

## Farm (on hold)

The owner put the farm off on 2026-11-22; it is not a separate feature now and nothing was worked on after
that. `farm/` keeps it as it was: a 400px band with sky, hills, trees, a fence, a shed whose window lights at
dusk, hay, crates and a pond with a jetty, on the life runtime as it stood then

## Judging

Three independent judgings so far. The second scored each set: cast 4, tools 4, emotions 4, conversations
3.5, locomotion 3.5, wardrobe 3.5, life 3.5 (3.7 overall). The third gave 4.2 overall, and the fourth round
of fixes answered its ranked list (no Moru standing in front of another, relocation after long stays,
in-between ears and wing in the runtime, roll face and soles, hand-typed raised flippers, the front turn,
worn pieces seated on turned poses). Nobody has judged the fourth round.

## Open items

- CPU: 5.5 to 6.1 % for 20 Moru in WebKit, over the 3 % ceiling; the cost is the GPU process. WebGL measured
  2.6 % (`life/browser/page-webgl.js`) and would need a decision record.
- 20 Moru in 1,920px cannot all stand clear: they still walk past each other and overlap on every tick (one
  cover lasted 82 s). Holding them in a row instead left 15 of 20 on their spot for ten minutes.
- Where nobody walks past anybody the order along the strip never changes, so a Moru only ever plays with its
  two neighbours, and about four approaches in ten are given up on the way.
- The raised flipper is three typed sprites, not a drawn key pose per clip; a turned outline is a little
  ragged and a worn piece on a turned pose loses its thin detail.
- No clip wears a cape, so only the runtime trails one. A room's Moru seldom leaves its place, since it walks
  only where the whole way is open.
- The wardrobe check does not dress the in-between ear and wing pictures. The cape is a flat wedge; the
  satchel has no strap.
- Tiptoe is the quietest move; pranks are rare (0 or 1 a minute with 8 Moru).
- The light background `#f4f1ea` is an assumed tone; nothing was tried in the app.
- Interaction catalogue rows 11 to 34 (greetings, hand-offs, argue and make up, race, hug and others) and
  the planned moves (hop, slide down, spin, tumble, prance) are not drawn.

## Build order into the app

1. Emotions: no layering needed, usable on every screen.
2. Anchor tracks in the manifest and the costume layer; horn, rabbit and scholar first.
3. Bubble layer and the reply-set picker; interactions 1 to 3 and 11 to 13.
4. Tool clips, one screen at a time, Work status first.
5. Locomotion and room-level travel.
6. Three-Moru moments and the remaining catalogue rows.
