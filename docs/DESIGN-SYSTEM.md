## Principles

- Atlas is a compact graph workbench, not a documentation portal with a graph attached: dark neutral surfaces, an achromatic frame with one calm blue for links and focus, indigo kept for the map's marks, dense readable controls. Dark only: no light switch, no `data-theme`.
- In the app, every mark, colour or effect carries a typed fact or a state; as gloss it is a regression. The public download page is a showcase: its effects, backgrounds and motion graphics need no fact behind them, only the smoothness and reduced-motion rules below. Motion may also be atmosphere (ambient light, breathing, drift) that loses to the data beside it. Gradients, glass, glow, scale hover, overshoot and further hues are allowed ("The expression bans are lifted", `docs/DECISIONS.md`) through a named token, with contrast floors and a reduced-motion equivalent held; every motion meets "Smoothness first" under "Motion".
- A control names the ontology handle it acts on (kind, slug, relation type, proof target, path) and what happens next. A screen that changes the graph exposes a copyable MCP packet or direct handoff so an agent can verify the change; query-like surfaces show result contracts, limits and partial evidence.
- The selected concept survives navigation: one canonical graph handle across map focus, detail, Workshop deep link and source-document handoff.
- A design change names the rule it serves or argues why it diverges. Lineage: [`docs/FOUNDATIONS.md` section 4](./FOUNDATIONS.md#4-design-lineage--restraint-as-craft-cited).

## Workbench composition and motion

Tokens keep screens related; they cannot decide what a screen is about. Name the **one fact or action** that must be understood at first glance, draw its real object or relationship at the largest useful scale, then place controls and evidence around it. If three equal panels can be swapped without changing the meaning, the composition is unfinished. A structure view draws its causal links; a table with decorative connectors is still a table.

An empty state shows the real zero state and the next action in one scene. A preview may show column names or a labelled vacant slot, never something resembling an existing result or agent run, and stays quieter than the action. An example of a result a destination gives is allowed only when it says "example" in words (on screen and in its accessible description), names which parts come from the person's data, and either rests on its finished frame after a bounded number of plays or keeps looping while visible and rests on that frame under reduced motion.

Motion explains *a named object*: selection reveals its evidence, a path highlights its real edges, and unrelated panels do not compete with it. Idle and ambient animation show a live fact or atmosphere, never an invented run or pending task. A screen recording, not a CSS value, proves continuity.

External design references are principles, not assets: no third-party screenshots, logos, fonts, code or recognizable compositions enter the repository.

## Surfaces and hierarchy

- **Order.** Primary task, then graph evidence (counts, relations, warnings, health, query readiness), then the next graph action. A large explanatory panel is never the first read; prefer compact handoff strips naming a destination and why. Retired structures (Browse/Write/Query cards, tree/ego hub, ERD Builder rails, query cockpit) are not revived.
- **Topology** (`/topology`) is the browse and decision surface: INDEX may project project, domain, capability, element, while canvas and datasheet keep typed relations, evidence and impact visible. `/ontology`, `/ontology/edit` and `/ontology/insights` are compatibility redirects, never second hubs.
- **Page rollout.** Pages inherit the map's language, not its canvas: chrome density (`--topology-chrome-*`), engraved mono numerals for counts that point to data, machined cards (`--color-panel`, 1px `--color-border-soft`, compact radius), hairline dividers (solid for contains, dashed for depends and stale), kind miniatures as legend markers, a powered dot for a real fresh or active state. Canvas-only marks: node shapes, comet pulse, diffraction spike, star dust, blueprint grid, vignette, hub ring, domain hull. Imitating them in DOM is an AI demo background. Tables use hairline dividers and right-aligned mono numbers, never zebra tint or a per-status badge palette.
- **Transient surfaces.** One surface owns the current action; support surfaces stay visibly weaker; blocking surfaces dim or suppress the rest. A new transient surface closes the previous unrelated one; a composer or modal demotes transient surfaces and blocks parent-map interaction, and a destructive confirm separates itself with a dim, scrim or blocked interaction. Elevation names layer order and nothing else. Chrome over a dark canvas uses dark panel tokens, never a light slab.
- **Colour is data, not atmosphere.** Kind colour (`--color-kind-*`) is allowed only where colour is the sole identity channel (the map's kind light and its legend dots); elsewhere a label, icon, size, position or legend carries kind (WCAG 1.4.1). Panels and cards stay neutral with compact swatches and low-alpha borders.
- **No left-edge selection stripe.** Never attach a coloured vertical line or full-height rail to a selected card, row or diagram part: it is a generic callout cue that says nothing about the part. Selection is the whole control's surface and boundary, its label, and the evidence or relation. Dense Git rows and the document outline have separately reviewed position markers.

## Topology node focus & scale

Overview first, zoom and filter, details on demand (`docs/design/topology-focus-and-scale.md`).

- **Click is ego focus plus a compact popover, never a fullscreen modal.** The node and its direct neighbours stay at full opacity and the rest dims through the world's focus-state derivation (the source graph is not mutated). A content-sized `OntologyMapDetailPanel` docks at the map's right inset (`selected-inspector-aligns-to-right-inset`), where it is a camera obstacle so the selected mark stays in view, and lists connected nodes as ego-walk targets; `FullDetail` is an explicit action inside it. On the Flat map a click selects and frames: the camera pans the selection and its lit reach between the panels and zooms in only while the far ends it showed stay in view; a domain frames its own star (disc, name, count line, member capabilities and their elements) instead and zooms in until that star fills the free map, never past the scale at which it still fits nor past the camera's deepest zoom, and the star's marks grow with the room they get, so partner domains may leave the view. INDEX stays open from 1280 px and temporarily folds below that width while the inspector is visible (explicit reopening still wins), and only the chrome under the panel fades where it stands. Every other source (double-click, Enter, search, INDEX, a panel link, a deep link, an arrow key) frames the node. One Escape clears the selection and the chrome, and returns the camera the selection moved from unless the reader has panned or zoomed since: a moved view is never taken back.
- **The default view is an overview**: project, domain and hub nodes at level 0, members on demand (semantic zoom). Never drop a 2-3k-node hairball on the person. A node-count capacity claim needs canvas-2D measurements at the stated viewport and interaction.
- **The canvas has no DOM.** Agents see it through `window.__atlasMap` (`?e2e=1` gate: typed node coordinates, camera, selection, cluster-chip claims; `map-testability`). Canvas 2D cannot read CSS variables: `src/widgets/ontology-map/tokens/read-map-tokens.ts` parses and caches them once.

## Node Spec

- **Kind is shape, not colour.** Hue cannot express order (Bertin) and is already spent on hierarchy (the brightness/depth ladder) and state (signal tones). Project is a hex plate, domain a square chip with pin ticks, capability a circle, element a square with a centre via-hole. A kind the map never draws (document, vault README, unknown) borrows no silhouette: the DOM facade draws the page mark (lucide `FileText`, quaternary ink). The full Flat map is the exception: it draws every kind as a disc and says kind by size, place and type (hub, large named domain disc, capability disc, element dot), and hue there carries domain territory, not kind.
- **One mapping, two gateways.** Canvas `render/node-shapes.ts` (silhouettes in `render/node-shapes/silhouette.ts`) and DOM `src/shared/ui/ontology-node/map-kind-glyph.tsx` render the same mapping (`node-kind-shape-parity`). Node icon sets (geometric, line) change render style only, never silhouettes. Thresholds live in the constant block at the top of `node-shapes.ts`: cite code by symbol, never by line, and never copy a threshold here.
- **Radius is a fixed ladder** (`--map-radius-{project,domain,capability,element}` via `radiusForKind()`), a design decision lint cannot judge. Changing it, `DOMAIN_HALF_EXTENT_RATIO` or adding a bridge-node visual reopens map research through a `code-reviewer` with the `map-marks` lens; a bridge visual must state what fact its light or border carries (red overlaps the error tone). 3D views size in screen pixels (`DOME_NODE_PX`), perspective only, never camera zoom.
- **Size** (`computeMagnitudeScale`): only domain and capability scale, as `1 + k(sqrt(childCount) - 1) / sqrt(maxChildCount)` clamped to 1..1.4, `k = --map-radius-magnitude-k`. Never below base: that would signal "small", and leaves have no countable children. Size encodes direct children, numbers encode descendants: an intentional dual channel.
- **Engraved numerals** (`drawEngraved`): project and domain only, above `ENGRAVED_COUNT_MIN_RADIUS`, faded in the overview; the value is `descendantCount`.

## Map marks and ink

- **Machined surface**: a 1px stroke over a neutral fill tier; canvas nodes allow one monochrome vertical sheen (`--map-node-sheen-tint`), DOM cards do not simulate it. **Engraved mono numerals** only for counts that point to real data (node, relation, census); decorative numbers are forbidden.
- **Signal means state.** Indigo is "powered" (fresh stroke, powered dot, active highlight); the comet pulse is live traversal of `depends`; stale is a dashed border on a low-chroma surface. Signal colour with no stated state is forbidden. Indigo is the resting protagonist and amber marks one hub ring; a further hue must name a typed fact or decision (tier, state, lens) through a `--color-*` token.
- **Chrome and canvas keep separate clocks.** The canvas moves on its own physics (spring camera, altitude crossfade, breathe; `src/widgets/ontology-map/model/motion-physics.ts`); chrome (`--topology-chrome-*`) moves on the shared `--motion-*` ramp and `SPRING`, never on a canvas timing.
- **Concept navigator.** Search, All/Recent tabs and one tree provide the path into the map. A row selects; its caret or arrow keys expand. Counts retain their membership scope; capacity meters and unexplained freshness dots are absent. The project inspector states the current source status once, keeps its remedy next to the gap, and discloses dated measurement details under Check record. Source verification never implies accepted product meaning.
- **Token scope.** `--map-*` is consumed only by the topology renderer and its panels, and the download page's film, which draws the Flat map's own marks and reads `--map-graph-*` through `readFlatGraphTokens` (plus its two numbers `--map-graph-film-growth`, its marks' size over the overview's, and `--map-graph-film-sink`, the ink share unrelated clusters keep while lit); any other page outside topology promotes the value to a global token first. Each map view spends only its own family through its own reader (Hex board `board/`, Flat dial `dial/tokens.ts`, Flat graph `flat-graph/tokens.ts`, the 3D tree `tokens/read-tree-tokens.ts`, light `light/`). The full Flat map mints no hue of its own: it rests achromatic on the neutral name ink, and colour belongs to attention — the project's ink `--map-graph-project` for the project and for the domain the pointer or selection sits in, the two directional line inks, and the signal tones. So the only colours on that canvas name a fact (`map-flat-graph-achromatic-rest.spec.ts`). The Flat graph's tunable values are `--map-graph-*` tokens in `app/styles/map-graph-tokens.css`, while fixed paint constants (focus link and ring widths, arrow spread, culling margin) stay named in `flat-graph/frame/paint.ts` and `frame/state.ts`: world spacing and ForceAtlas2 settings, disc radii (capability radius grows with the square root of its element count between - **Labels** take their baseline from one function (`resolveLabelBaselineY` in `render/labels.ts`) shared by draw and bbox build, anchor to the drawn screen radius, keep `LABEL_NODE_CLEARANCE` from the outline, and reserve every drawn disc (selected and hovered win). A blocked label flips first and is suppressed last, so no shape goes nameless.
- **Side lanes** `--map-safe-inset-left/right` are the chrome standing on that side plus one equal air. Cluster controls share one decision function for draw, hit-test and label scheduling (`clusterControlForm`) and never overlap (`expand-settings`). Canvas backgrounds are static, capped at `--canvas-bg-ink-max`, and lose to data.

## Design tokens

### Where values live

`app/globals.css` imports the parts in `app/styles/` in order (`@theme static` ramps in `tokens.css`, other global `:root` tokens in `base-root-alpha.css`, `--map-*` in `base-map-tokens.css`); gates read the joined text through `readGlobalCss()` (`scripts/lib/design/global-css.mjs`).

- Every colour is a CSS variable, declared once. `@theme static` emits every theme value to `:root` whether or not a class names it, so a theme token never gets a second `:root` copy.
- A control that turns the browser outline off uses `outline-hidden` (`focus-visible:outline-hidden` beside its ring), never `outline-none`: under `forced-colors` the box-shadow ring is not painted and `outline-hidden` keeps a 2px outline in its place. `outline-none` stays only where focus is moved for announcement (a dialog, panel or heading with `tabIndex={-1}` and no ring) or is drawn inside a canvas.
- A token needs a living consumer, and a token this document cites must exist (`design-doc-token-integrity`).
- A new topology token states its product reason, state or layer, responsive fallback and a WebView or test marker.
- Lint blocks raw hex and colour functions, `text-[Npx]`, `rounded-[Npx]`, hand-built shadows, numeric `duration-*` and z-index 20+ without `--z-*`. Spacing is deliberately unenforced.

### Scale fixed contract

Deviation is a defect.

- Chrome fields, tiles and chips are `--chrome-tile-size` tall; chrome labels use `text-body`, the step every other 36px control wears; the rail icon is one step, `--app-nav-rail-icon-size`.
- No UI zoom below 2400px; `.topology-ui-scale` is 1.1 only from 2400px.
- Faces: self-hosted Pretendard Variable (`--font-sans`) and JetBrains Mono (`--font-mono`); weights 400, 510, 560, 650 only. No CJK font file ships (`app/styles/lang-cjk.css` names installed OS faces). Every page draws Latin, digits and symbols from Pretendard's upstream subsets (`src/app/fonts.css`); Korean pages draw Hangul from all 92 upstream dynamic subsets as one family, `Pretendard Variable` (`src/app/main.tsx`, `app/styles/lang-cjk.css`), so a page fetches only the subsets its characters fall in and the whole 2 MB file only for a character no subset carries. Canvas text loads the faces of the text it draws (`src/widgets/ontology-map/render/product-glyphs.ts`).
- A size step carries its own leading (the companion, for example `--text-body--line-height`); changing only the size breaks the pair. Text that inherits the 16px root has escaped the ramp.
- Every type, leading and radius step is registered in `cn()` (`TYPE_RAMP_STEPS`, `LEADING_RAMP_STEPS`, `RADIUS_RAMP_STEPS` in `src/shared/lib/cn.ts`); an unregistered step is read as a colour or size class and silently dropped or left unmerged.

### Colour

- **Surfaces** step `--color-canvas`, `--color-panel`, `--color-elevated`; hover and selection washes are `--color-overlay-1..3`; borders are `--color-border-soft` (default), `--color-divider`, `--color-border-strong`.
- **Text ink**: primary, secondary, tertiary (7:1 on canvas and panel, so every text at 11 px and below starts here), quaternary (12.5 px and up only). Quaternary clears 4.5:1 only on the bare surfaces (canvas, panel, elevated) and panel plus overlay-1; hover or selection (panel plus overlay-2), elevated plus overlay-1 or more, and indigo or amber tint composites use tertiary, deeper composites secondary. A clickable row never rests on quaternary (`quaternary-ink-surface`). `--color-text-on-accent` (dark ink) is the only foreground on the filled primary action (`controlClass({ tone: 'onAccent' })`).
- **Chrome is achromatic; hue belongs to data.** Surfaces and ink are warm graphite neutrals. The primary action is a paper-white fill (`--color-indigo-brand`) with dark ink, and the tint and line ladders (`pnpm design:tokens -- --prefix=--color-indigo`) are neutral alphas of that paper white, so selection and hover carry no hue. One calm blue (`--color-indigo-accent`, with `--color-indigo-text-soft`) is the ink for links, focus and sparks, and is AA on every base and tint (`accent-ink-contrast`). Kind colours, signal tones and the map keep their own hues. The `indigo` token names are historical; there is no accent setting. Spell translucent indigo only with the existing alpha-step tokens (`pnpm design:tokens -- --prefix=--color-indigo`): a hand-copied `rgba()` of a token passes the raw-colour gate and is still a defect.
- **Signal tones**: warning amber, error red, success emerald, each a solid dot plus a symmetric surface, border and text alpha ladder on one hue. They explain state, never decoration; success marks a real positive state only. No fourth tone without a charter update.
- **Three ambers, three rules** (a grep for "amber" finds unrelated families). *Hub amber* `--map-amber-hub`: one hub ring, one Layer-0 container, two written exceptions (agent focus ring, `?recent=` spotlight); a third on screen is a defect. *Kind tone* `--color-kind-<kind>-rgb`: a data mark; the canvas reads the token itself (`read-map-tokens.ts`), so there is no paint copy to drift. *Footprint trail* `--color-footprint-trail`: deliberately not the hub value, so "centre" and "walked" never merge (`footprint-trail-ink`).
- **Bars are neutral plus one indigo**: indigo marks the primary series only, and segments separate by a 1px seam, not by hue (indigo against neutral is 1.12:1, under the 3:1 graphic threshold). The one exception is a source's state (Library Sources home, `src/widgets/library-sources-overview/model/source-states.ts`): one `--color-source-*` ink per state, the same in the band's bar, the row bars, the legend and the panel's words, each at least 3:1 on canvas and panel (`design-status-token-contrast.test.ts`). Read in part is hatched, so it never depends on hue against has a page; every bar keeps its 1px seams and sits beside its numbers or state words, and a fully done bar turns `--color-source-complete` once. Text never wears `--color-source-none`. **Texture is not fact**: an ambient surface is monochrome in a neutral ramp, carries no count, title or link, and never wears signal inks.
- **Work status rooms and session inks** (`app/styles/work-status-tokens.css`, `src/widgets/work-tracks/`): the building of rooms is drawn on one pixel grid in flat neutral `--work-room-*` fills on the page's own canvas, with no slab behind the rooms: `--work-room-wall` one step above the canvas, `--work-room-screen` the canvas's black again so a screen reads as a window and the page keeps two greys, near-black for pits, steel for the cart and the stop at the end of a room's rail, crate greys by kind (`-crate`, `-crate-taped` for a lock left, `-crate-dusty` for a worktree no one holds, `-crate-ghost` for a missing folder) and `--work-shelf-crate` and `--work-shelf-crate-old` for the flat squares that count the shelf. Each agent session takes one ink, `--work-session-1` to `--work-session-5` in start order (ordered so the first sessions sit furthest apart in hue), for the 3px top edge a stage room's wall carries while it travels, the hairline along the top of its floor plate and mini rooms, its floor marker, the mark on the screen's bezel, the marks of its last hour's changes on that bezel and the rail of a room whose work runs; a worktree no agent holds is `--work-unheld`. The inks are the fact "which session holds this" and never fill a wall or a screen, clear 3:1 on the room walls, and none is a signal tone: a stuck room says so in error red words. `--work-ground-server` and `--work-seam` stay for the web notice's art. A room's light is mixed from its light colour (session ink, `--color-status-warning` for a room that needs a person, `--color-status-success` for a merge) at the `--work-room-glow-*` strengths for the soft light inside its frame, which reaches `--work-room-glow-reach` and lies under the room's words, and the `--work-room-rim-*` strengths for the frame's hairline, brightest along the top and fading down the sides; `--work-room-spark-length` is the pulse's spark. The ladder runs rest, fresh, alert, flare, complete, so a merge is the brightest light on the page.
- **Paper** (`Sheet`, `src/shared/ui/marks/sheet.tsx`): a file drawn as a 28×34 sheet, about two lines of its row's text tall, decoration beside its name. `--color-paper-source` is the elevated surface with `--color-paper-line` (overlay-3) lines, `--color-paper-edge` the strong border and `--color-paper-fold` the paper-white a42 step on the folded corner, 3:1 against the source paper. A wiki page is written paper with no hue: `--color-paper-page` mixes 62% of the primary ink into the elevated surface, `--color-paper-page-fold` 38% for its edge and fold, `--color-paper-page-title` (canvas a88) draws its title line and `--color-paper-line-page` (canvas a34) its body lines. The format word is `text-caption` in tertiary ink on the source paper, and a page's source tab is secondary ink on the canvas inside the sheet's height; both are AA (`design-status-token-contrast`).

### Type ramp

Steps: caption, label, body, body-lg, title, reading, display, hero, hero-lg, monument (`pnpm design:tokens -- --prefix=--text-`).

- Declared in `rem`, so a browser's text-only zoom reaches type; pixels at the default root are the ramp's numbers. Chrome geometry (tile, rail and dock widths, hit floors, dialog tiers) stays `px`: type follows the reader, boxes do not. A chrome box that stays absolute while its padding, icon and label follow the root declares a clip or collapse rule.
- `reading` is authored-document body on `--leading-prose`. It equals `title` in size and differs in kind: `--measure-doc-column` derives from it, so swapping them moves the reading column.
- Snap an off-ramp size to the nearest step; exactly between goes up. A step that does not exist (`text-large`) emits no CSS and renders at the 16px root (`type-ramp-step-defined`). A new step needs the ramp in `tokens.css` and registration in `cn.ts`.
- JavaScript that resolves a size token (canvas `ctx.font` cannot take `var()`) uses `rootFontPx()` and `cssLengthToPx()` from `src/shared/ui/tokens/root-font-size.ts`: `parseFloat("0.6875rem")` is 0.6875, 16 times too small.
- `rem` in a media query resolves against the browser's default font size, not `html { font-size }`: test text zoom of type by setting the root in script, but of layout only in a browser launched with `defaultFontSize` (`text-zoom-layout.spec.ts`; gate `type-ramp-root-relative`).

### Line-height ramp

Line height is a pair of its size, on a 2px grid in `rem`, plus ratio steps (`--leading-display-tight`, `-prose`, `-monument`).

- UI text uses its size's pair (carried by `text-<step>`); authored text uses `prose`; names and numbers that read as one block use `display-tight`, at most two lines (three make Hangul final consonants touch the line below).
- Korean sets the upper limit and Latin follows; no locale branching, because one paragraph mixes both.
- `text-[length:var(--text-display)]` keeps the size and loses the pair (`arbitrarySizeSelectors`). A conditional size (`sm:text-...`) misaligns the pair: use ramp utilities for it or an explicit `leading-*` covering both.
- In `cn()` write size first, line height second: tailwind-merge lets a later `text-<step>` absorb a preceding `leading-*`.

### Icons, radius, shadow

- **Icons.** Content icons beside text use `ICON_SIZE` (`src/shared/ui/tokens/icon-size.ts`: sm, md, lg), mirrored by `--icon-*` in `rem` because lucide's `size` prop cannot read `var()`. Pair the step with its type: sm with label and body (default), md with body-lg, lg with title.
- **Radius**: micro (kbd, micro badge), chip (chips, fields, `Select` trigger, `Button` at every size), card (items nested in panels, repeated list items), panel (sections, popovers, modals), sheet. Sheet is only for large transient surfaces that float and obscure what is below (shortcut, search and agent-connection sheets); inflow content never wears it. Zones placed directly on a page, sheet or drawer are panel plus `--card-pad`; boxes nested in them are card. Hairline meter tracks keep 1-4px; other exceptions stay `eslint-disable` with a reason.
- **Shadow**: three float tiers (`--shadow-elevation-1` toolbars, card hover, toast; `-2` popovers and palettes; `-3` dialog), two docking tiers for surfaces attached to a screen edge (`-dock-bottom`, `-dock-side`: the downward tiers would cast off-screen) and `--shadow-control-press`, and `--shadow-paper`, a `filter` of two `drop-shadow`s on the coach-mark alpha that sets a `Sheet` on its row (a filter, so the folded corner keeps its shadow). Lint checks which token a value uses, not the presence of `var(`. Never hand-write a drop shadow; a new role is a token PR first. An inset hairline is material, not a shadow.

### Measures and page frames

- **Prose measure** `--measure-prose` is a `ch` cap on one line of authored text, applied to prose elements inside a column, never to the column; `ch` is the advance of `0`, not a character, so calibrate by rendering. **Document column** `--measure-doc-column` is that measure spent at `--text-reading` plus two gutters, so box and line cannot disagree; it grows with the reader's text size and from 1536px wide (Tailwind `2xl`) the measure spends 78 steps instead of 66 so the column keeps about half of a 1920 pane (`base-responsive.css`). Consumers that cannot read CSS variables (popout stylesheet, image `sizes`, the outline rail's fit verdict) use `src/shared/ui/tokens/reading-measure.ts`.
- **Page frames** (`src/shared/ui/tokens/page-frame.ts`) follow the direction the eye travels. Text and settings (fill a field, read a sentence, act on a row) use `PAGE_FRAME_FORM`, the 960px column centred at every width, as Agents and Automations do (owner, 2026-10-16); a canvas (map, graph, diagram) or a list of many short cards uses `PAGE_FRAME` (`--page-max`) or none. Every rail destination but the map draws `PageHeaderBar` on the `page` gutter (Harness on its own container, the same pixel), so the title holds one x and y across the rail; the bar is chrome, and the body beneath keeps its own frame (2026-10-21). A body holding one stage (an app-only card, an empty lane, at most one project, a setup step) wears `PAGE_STAGE_FILL` and its stage `my-auto`, which centres the stage on the shell slot; a list starts under the bar. A destination with nothing to show yet draws `FeatureStage` (`src/shared/ui/status/feature-stage.tsx`): one centred column of an artwork that depicts what the feature does, its title, one sentence and the one way in. The artwork arrives once within `--motion-choreography` and answers hover or focus on the action. It may then keep acting what the feature does as an ambient loop; a stage that does passes `artLoops`, and `FeatureStage` draws the shared pause (`MotionToggle`) under the artwork in a zero-height row, so the title, sentence and action do not move. `artWidth="wide"` gives a scene of several parts a wider artwork box. The artwork stands on a plate the stage draws: a dot grid and one indigo light, both masked out before the column's edge; the light brightens while the action is hovered or focused. A `WaitStage` whose box starts under the bar wears `PAGE_WAIT_LIFT`, so every page wait shares the route fallback's centre. A destination's `h1` wears `PAGE_TITLE` and its lede `PAGE_LEDE`; call sites add placement only (`page-frame`). Changing a frame value is a specification change that owes a decision record. A body mixing an `mx-auto` column with left-fixed surfaces has two alignment origins that diverge with the viewport.
- **Gateway** has one alignment origin, `--gateway-origin`, the only gateway token registered through `@property <length>` because JavaScript reads it (and re-derives on resize). `--gateway-page-max` stays at least `--page-max`. The download hero's app window alone leaves the column: from 80rem it runs to 2rem from the screen's edge, and stacked it takes the screen's width (`.gateway-hero-window`). Never centre with `mx-auto`: it creates a second origin.
- **Widths have no shared unit**: they derive from content or geometry (`min()`, `clamp()`), not scale steps. For a new surface width, reuse a same-nature token, else add a surface-specific token with a derivation comment in that surface's block; that needs no approval. A one-off `max-w-*` is an optical correction. **Padding is not a ramp**: `--card-pad` is the card and panel inset, `--map-panel-pad` is map-local, and `gap-*` and `space-*` use the 4/8 steps.

### Touch & tablet responsive contract

Touch is its own axis, not a width. `--touch-target-min` promotes control heights, the chrome tile, rail tile and overlay close under the coarse-pointer queries in `app/styles/`; fine pointers keep density. Below `lg` the `BottomTabBar` exists: a surface anchored to the bottom or ending in a scroll area reserves `--topology-mobile-bottom-tab-reserve`, and "blocked by the tab bar" is a defect. Below `md` the expanded INDEX is a full-bleed sheet.

## Controls

### Components and layers

`/ui-build` holds the need-to-component table. The **value layer** (`controlClass()`, `fieldClass()`, `fieldLabel()`, `badgeClass()` in `src/shared/ui/`) owns appearance, size, colour and disabled state; the **action layer** (`Chip`, `IconButton` with a required `label`, `RowButton`, `Input`, `Textarea`, `Checkbox`) owns what a string cannot carry: `type="button"` by default (a button in a form submits), accessible names, error wiring, keyboard semantics. `Button` is primary, outline, ghost or danger (the confirm step of an irreversible action), always at the chip radius; a page's one way forward is a `primary` Button, never a `controlClass` pill, which carries a state or count. `Dialog` is blocking (scrim, trap, Escape, return, scroll lock, `--z-dialog`) and comes in four sizes: `sm` and `md` prompts (`--dialog-w-sm`, `--dialog-w-md`), `lg` reading sheets (`--dialog-w-lg`, at most the viewport minus `--chrome-inset` tall, the consumer's column scrolling under a fixed header, and the `viewport` frame once 960px no longer fits) and `viewport` work surfaces; non-modal surfaces use `Surface`. `CloseButton` is the one close control. Raw `<select>`, `role="dialog"`, text fields, `<details>`, `type="checkbox"` and `role="tab"` outside their primitive are held by adoption ratchets that only fall.

### Control shapes and heights

`controlClass()` has eight shapes (chip, link, row, icon, pill, card, tile, segment), derived from a census of production buttons. Axes: `scope: 'app' | 'panel'` (the panel's achromatic ink lamp, `--map-panel-text-*`), `tone` (including `onAccent`, paired with its filled background), `truncate`. A new axis, tone or shape needs the count of controls it leaves outside the system; an exception axis is a symptom of a wrong value, so fix the value.

Heights come from one ladder, and a height outside it is a deviation: 24 (no token; the WCAG 2.5.8 floor), 28 `--control-h-sm`, 32 `--control-h-md` (default), 36 `--chrome-tile-size`, 40 `--control-h-lg`, 44 `--touch-target-min`.

- The ladder defines height and padding fits inside it, never the reverse.
- Single-line horizontal shapes (chip, pill, segment, row, card) and squares (icon) declare a ladder `min-h-*`, never a hard `h-*` (it clips wrapped controls). `xs` lowers inset and radius but keeps the 24 floor and its type.
- **Type follows height.** A control's resting height picks its type: `label` at 24, `body` from 28 to 36, `body-lg` at 40 and 44. The value layer, `Button` and `Select` carry the pair, so two controls of one height never differ in type; a multi-line field takes the step of its floor, so text is typed at the size it is read back in. A call site names a type step only beside a height it sets by hand, and then that height's step. On a row or a card a hand `min-h` is a floor under content that may stack, judged only beside a hand type step. Outside the rule: `link` (it reads at the size of the text around it), `icon` and `tile` (no label inside the box), `TabBar` labels (`body` at every strip height) and the coarse-pointer floor, which raises a control to 44 and leaves its type.
- Vertically stacked controls (tile, rail item) take the ladder on their inner tile, not the sum with label and gap. Inline prose links are exempt. A height not on the ladder means fixing the ladder, not adding an axis (`control-class`).

### Links, touch floor, disabled

- The `link` floor is 24 (`min-h-6`). Coarse pointers reach 44 by actual height (`.atlas-touch-floor` on chip, row, pill) or hit expansion (`.touch-hit-expand`, a coarse-only pseudo-element for icon and hand-drawn controls, only with at least 12px clearance, or a later `::after` steals the earlier tap). The floor rule sits outside cascade layers, and its query is `any-pointer: coarse` (a touchscreen laptop reports `pointer: fine`).
- Links inside markdown flow are not controls: they wear `.prose-link` (underline geometry only).
- `CONTROL_DISABLED_CLASS` (`control-class.ts`) is the one disabled treatment: opacity 55, `not-allowed`, no shadow, no hover; dimming alone leaves a control that looks disabled and still reacts. Elements that cannot take `disabled:` use `opacity-55` directly (`disabled-affordance`).

### Fields

`fieldClass({ frame, size, multiline })`. `boxed` is a record field (the value persists, so the input wins); `bare` is a query field (the result wins) and emits no dimensions, border, radius, inset or touch floor, because the parent provides the box. `size` maps 1:1 to the ladder (`xs` and `sm` 28, `md` 32, `lg` 40) and type follows the height (`text-body` at 28 and 32, `text-body-lg` at 40). A record form whose typed value must outrank its label uses `lg`. A stage-size input (a 23px name on a creation card) is out of spec because its size carries information. `fieldLabel({ row })`: a name label is `text-label` in secondary ink, never smaller than its own footnote; a whole-row clickable label takes the touch floor and `min-h-6` (the label is the target); a layout-only wrapper gets no styling.

### Select, empty state, tabs, scrollers

- **Select** renders its list in a portal (an absolute list is clipped by ancestor `overflow: hidden`) and caps it at `LISTBOX_MAX_ROWS` or the remaining space, whichever is smaller, with a scroll affordance only when the cap clips (`select-growth.ts`).
- **EmptyState**: icon, skeleton, title, description, action; `tone` dashed (inside lists and cards) or solid; `align` left or centre. A centred card without a description demotes its title to the body step; with a left icon the action row sits in the text column. Empty charts and lists show a filling form (skeleton), not long whitespace.
- **TabBar** serves panel navigation (document sections, agent inbox categories); closable file tabs, preview and edit modes and search filters are other jobs. A flat row at least `--control-h-lg` tall with a 2px bottom indicator (`--tabbar-underline`) and a stronger primary label: no selected fill, raised frame, top cue or rounded silhouette. Tabs are `shrink-0 whitespace-nowrap` (a flex child in `overflow-x-auto` otherwise wraps), the active tab scrolls into view on mount and resize, and the overflow fade (`--tabbar-edge-fade`) draws only on the side with hidden tabs.
- **Scrollers** paint no bar: `scrollbar-width: none` and `::-webkit-scrollbar { display: none }` on `html`, `body` and every descendant (both spellings: Safari and the macOS WebView ignore the standard one). A hidden bar removes the only "there is more" mark, so a scroller with no other cue pairs with a `--tabbar-edge-fade` mask. `.atlas-scroll-quiet` marks scrollers designed without one. `scroll-behavior: smooth` is on `html` only, under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`; on inner boxes it changes what `scrollTop = n` means.

A panel (INDEX, datasheet) is one radius step above. Never rebuild a 36px square or label button inline.

## Surface contracts

- **Shell body slot.** Scroll containers do not compress children: `AppShell` declares `[&>*]:shrink-0` once, because page roots use `min-h-full` and flex would otherwise shrink the page box and trap its bottom reserve. A rule pages must remember fails on the next screen (`AppShell.test.tsx`; jsdom cannot see layout).

- **Column existence promises content existence** (Git destination, `--git-*`). Render a column only when it has content; the work area is `min(content, remaining space)` (flex `0 1 auto` with `min-h-0`, never `flex-1`); two columns start at `xl`, because at `lg` the evidence column crushes the list and a truncated list is not decision material.

A warning dot marks only the page's own defect: a warning ink every row wears marks nothing. The ambient surfaces (`src/views/library/expressive/`) split fact from texture: the constellation stands beside the copy, never under it; the synapse field is texture behind the working pane. Both sleep through `ambientSleepFactor`, freeze under reduced motion, and are `aria-hidden` and `pointer-events-none`. The spine folds below `--library-spine-collapse-height`, a height of its own pane (the literal repeats in the `@container` rule; `library-spine.spec.ts` reads the token). The Ontology reader has two chrome bands: the destination tabs, with the documents/sets switch behind a divider while Ontology is open, and the file-tab strip, whose part over the list carries the folder chip and the list's search, order and new tools as 36px tiles. The reading column owns its head: the file path as a mono eyebrow, the document's name as the page h1 at `display` when the body has no H1 of its own, and the file doors, the preview/edit switch and the sync state ending on the column's edge. An empty ledger draws its first row as a ghost in the real row geometry under real column headers, not as a legend.
- **Wiki question report** is an open document surface with thin rules, not a stack of cards: the question is the one visible headline, source addresses stay visible beside an actionable citation, a zero local word match is a retrieval limit and never a claim the originals lack an answer, and an unreviewed draft never looks like accepted Wiki meaning.
- **Contextual ontology writing** is on `/topology`: a selected node's inspector swaps in place into a one-relation editor, and creation and ACP writes share one typed pre-write review. The map is the preview (a dashed arrow on live endpoint geometry, never layout input). `OntologyChangeReview` shows exact fields before `createDoc`, `updateFrontmatter` or ACP `allow_once`; semantic writes never offer `allow_always`.
- **Architecture canvas** (the flow surface) sets each role as one face in rank order: a top-to-bottom ladder that pairs the approved contract face with its observation face across a 72–160px delta gutter (it grows with the canvas) when the rows fit, a left-to-right chain when they do not. Every face is the same rounded rectangle; a role's declared ends (`allow_*`, `dependency_policy`) budget its caption, never its outline. A stroke carries only what the order cannot: under `lower-only` the six adjacent spine strokes are drawn and the skips are not; `explicit` edges and measured traffic are drawn (`edgeSource` says which). On a lower-only ladder the rule is said once under the contract heading (`dependencyLowerOnly`) instead of once per stroke; per-gap sentences return as measured counts. ## Motion

Motion is allowed broadly: slow, expressive, ambient and choreographed motion, hover that lifts, scales, tilts or glows, routes and tabs that travel, lists that stagger. Hover and focus still finish within `--motion-settle` (lint): a hover that takes longer reads as lag, not smoothness. Three things stay binding, in this order: smoothness, reduced motion, and named values. "Allocation and swaps" items 1–3 and "Signature motion", except its light rule, are guidance a design review weighs, not gates.

### Smoothness first

The acceptance bar for any motion is that it runs smoothly. A motion that misses one of these is a defect, however good it looks in a still.

1. **Frame budget.** No frame interval above 50 ms while it runs: on the map at synth 2k on first open, measured with `expectSmoothMotion` (`tests/e2e/motion-sampler.ts`). The bar is enforced headed on the static export with `MAP_PERF_BARS=1` (`docs/engineering/motion-recording.md`); CI enforces the rules below and prints the measured interval.
2. **Compositor properties.** Movement animates `transform` (with `translate`, `scale`, `rotate`), `opacity`, `filter` and `clip-path`; paint (colour, border, background) may transition once per input; loops stay compositor-only. A layout property (`width`, `height`, `top`, `left`, margins, padding) never animates in a loop; a one-shot reflow (inline expansion height, dock width, the selection indicator's size) runs once per input and is declared to `expectSmoothMotion`. A transition names the property it animates. An infinite CSS animation animates compositor properties only, with one licensed exception: a pixel sprite or a text-clipped gradient moved by `background-position`, once its keyframe has a line in `PAINT_LICENSED`; a scripted `repeat: Infinity` loop lists its file in `SCRIPTED_LOOPS` (both in the `infinite-motion` contract). Adding that line is the route, not a reason to avoid the loop.
3. **Interruptible.** Motion never blocks input: the next pointer, key or wheel input settles or retargets it, and a retarget starts from the displayed value and velocity (`springState`). A staggered entrance settles on the first input (`useStaggerOnce`; `ProjectCard.tsx` and the git `StepList` stagger are known exceptions to migrate to it). A route view transition holds input while it runs, so it stays within `--motion-base` (`route-transition-input.spec.ts`); it may travel inside that time.
4. **Rest is free.** The map returns to zero frames at rest (`map-sleep-frames.spec.ts`); it may animate longer after an event and then settles. Ambient and infinite motion pauses when the document is hidden or the element is offscreen: CSS loops follow `BrandWaitingMark` (`animation-play-state: paused` until a `data-*-motion="running"` attribute set from an `IntersectionObserver` and `visibilitychange`), canvas loops sleep through `ambientSleepFactor` 30 s after the last input, and a loop that shows running work stops with the work (`infinite-motion` lists these work-only loops). Ambient, decorative and character motion (Moru, [Brand](design/brand.md#contextual-motion)) may loop for as long as it is visible; it has no time limit. The download page, the one public page a visitor reaches before choosing anything, offers one visible control that pauses all its loops (WCAG 2.2.2): `MotionToggle` (`src/shared/ui/controls/motion-toggle.tsx`) over the store in `src/shared/motion/motion-pause.ts`. The choice is remembered in this browser; a paused loop holds its frame and continues from it; reduced motion stops the loops too and the control is not offered. Inside the product no screen draws a pause: reduced motion, which the Screen settings row reports, is the stop control. Tailwind's `animate-spin` and `animate-pulse` are compositor loops for running work only.
5. **No layout shift.** Motion moves no other content outside a declared reflow; hover motion moves an inner layer, so the hit box stays where the pointer is.
6. **Continuous values.** A value that moves continues from the value last drawn, and from its speed when the motion can be retargeted: no size, position, opacity or zoom changes by more than one frame's share in a single frame, a state change crossfades or holds its box, a value that arrives in events (a download's progress) is interpolated, and a late frame moves a value no further than a normal frame would. Measured per frame in the running product, not in a unit of the easing (`map-flat-graph-star-motion.spec.ts`).

### Ramp and defaults

- Durations are named, never typed: `--motion-fast` (a state that already happened: hover, focus, colour, tab content), `--motion-base` (a surface changing position), `--motion-settle` (a finished change), `--motion-slow` (large travel: a sheet, a tab panel that travels, or a route's arriving content after the view transition releases input; routes themselves stay within `--motion-base`; a surface crossing the window), `--motion-expressive` (an expressive entrance or morph, and the ceiling of a staggered list), `--motion-choreography` (the whole budget of a rare multi-part entrance; no entrance runs longer, and a loop's period is a multiple of a step such as `calc(var(--motion-base) * 24)`, not a new token). Values: `pnpm design:tokens -- --prefix=--motion-`; the JS mirror is `MOTION` in `src/shared/motion/tokens.ts` (`motion-token-mirror`). Named exceptions keep their reason (`--agent-panel-reflow-duration`, `--overlay-spring-response`).
- A new duration, easing or spring is a named token or `SPRING` entry with a line here and a consumer or a named first use; it needs no decision record. Lint refuses numeric `duration-*` and literal times in animation shorthands; framer and inline styles take `MOTION`, `SPRING` or a `var(--motion-*)`.
- `--motion-ease` is for every entry. `--motion-ease-exit` is for exits only, on the clock `calc(var(--motion-base) * 0.67)`: an exit on the entry curve is the entrance played backwards. It never appears in a `transition:` (a transition runs both ways); JavaScript reaches it only through `EXIT_TRANSITION` (`motion-token-mirror`). `--motion-ease-place` is for arrivals that are set down: transform only, on a figure with no type inside, never on a surface or anything carrying a sentence.
- Tailwind's default transition duration and easing are `--motion-fast` and `--motion-ease` (set in `@theme`): write no duration class when the default is meant. Hover and focus take a step up to `--motion-settle`; the longer steps are for travel and entrances (lint). Duration and easing move as one family.
- Entrance distance is a name: `OVERLAY_RISE` and `SHEET_RISE` in `src/shared/motion/tokens.ts`. Camera and drag timings are canvas physics (`src/widgets/ontology-map/model/motion-physics.ts`); a DOM surface never references them.

### Allocation and swaps

1. **The protagonist moves first.** When one input changes several surfaces, the transition belongs to the attention winner; a hard-cut winner (first-frame delta share above 70%) over an easing background is inverted allocation. Both instant is fine.
2. **One input reads as one event.** Stages that answer one input start together; a staggered or choreographed sequence is one event with one total time on a named step, and any input settles it.
3. **Verdicts are measured in frames**, not screenshots; a design verdict names the winner's transition token.
4. **An exit plays under its own animation name**: a `reverse` on the same element does not restart and shows the end state instantly (`exit-motion-restart`). Opacity-only classes use `--motion-ease`, never a movement curve.
5. **A swap keeps both frames** (`usePanelPresence`, `useSurfaceSwap`, `useSwapHeight` in `src/shared/lib/render-timing/use-presence.ts`): the departing surface stays for one shared `EXIT_WINDOW_MS`, inert and `pointer-events-none`, and a crossfade wraps reflow by measuring height just before replacement.
6. **Inline expansion is one surface with two states**: the header band is constant and the detail grows; open, close and content swap share one transition (height `--motion-base`, content `--motion-fast`: space first on open, text clears first on close). The closing detail is `inert`.

Frequency still matters: a surface met dozens of times a day is where a long or loud motion costs most, so give it the shorter step unless the motion carries something the reader needs.

### Reduced motion

The OS preference is the only switch. The global kill rule (`animation-duration: 0.01ms !important`, inside `@layer base`) is the safety net for un-audited, decorative or infinite motion. Motion that is evidence of causality gets an equivalent, not a kill: crossfades reclaim their duration token, and appearances with transforms switch to the opacity-only `panelCrossfadeIn` keyframe; no new keyframe, no literal milliseconds. The surfaces that owe an equivalent are an enforced list (`reduced-motion-equivalent`), and an equivalent sits in the same cascade layer as the kill rule and later in source order (between important declarations layer order inverts, so one outside the layer loses). Springs do not travel and the light layer is never created. User-initiated movement (scroll, pan, pinch, wheel zoom) keeps its duration (WCAG 2.2 section 2.3.3); only app-initiated movement (ego dive, fit, auto-align) snaps. Every new primitive, ambient loop or travelling transition ships its equivalent in the same part (opacity only, or none); `reduced-motion-equivalent` derives its list from the CSS, so a new animated class is on it from its first line. Ambient motion stands still on the rest style its rule declares.

### Motion primitives

A growing library: each primitive owns its `app/styles/motion-*.css` part inside `@layer base` or a module in `src/shared/motion`, carries its reduced equivalent, and has a line below. Durations, easings and distances come from `--motion-*`, `MOTION`, `SPRING` and `STAGGER` only. A new primitive needs no record.

- **Stage pulse** (`FeatureStagePulse`, `src/shared/ui/status/feature-stage.tsx`): one short light travels a line of a feature stage's artwork over `--motion-choreography`, once after the line is drawn and once more each time the stage's action is hovered or focused. Reduced motion draws no pulse.

### Signature motion

Conduction is Atlas's own character, kept as guidance: meaning travels along real connections, with weight that depends on structure.

1. Motion caused by a relation runs along that relation's own line, in its direction; nothing crosses empty space. Everything arrives from its origin and leaves toward it.
2. Light marks what is happening or what is held: an act for as long as it lasts, or a persistent state (selected, active, running) on a named token. Ambient light is atmosphere and loses to data.
3. Weight follows structure: speed depends on scale (control, surface, canvas) and, on the map, on mass (a concept's degree). Heavy concepts arrive later, never bounce more.

Spend the loudest motion where it is rarest: hover and typing take ink or a small lift, a press or tab change a spring, an answered question a spring plus light, a first open or a landed change a choreography within `--motion-choreography`. This is a starting point, not a gate.

- **Springs**: `SPRING` in `src/shared/motion/spring.ts` (`control`, `surface`, `canvas` at `HOUSE_DAMPING_RATIO`); `springState` is the closed form, retargetable from current position and velocity. A new named spring is a `SPRING` entry, with its own damping ratio if it needs one, a row in `spring.test.ts` and a line here; no record. DOM consumers get `springEasing(spring)`, a CSS `linear()` generated at runtime: no static CSS spring token and no hand-typed `linear()` (`motion-token-mirror`). A spring moves position, size, rotation or scale; opacity, colour, blur and light intensity never overshoot and stay on the ramp.
- **Light** on the map: the WebGL2 light layer marks only the thing a hand or an agent is acting on, for as long as the act lasts, never a mark with identity; it draws in Flat for an event and nothing at rest. It composites `plus-lighter` only there; everything else is `source-over`. It reads `--map-light-*`, borrows `--map-indigo-bright`, adds no hue, and rides the front of the relation's reveal. A persistent glow elsewhere is DOM or canvas-2D paint on a named token; a glow `box-shadow` adds its shadow token family to lint's shadow allowlist with its first consumer.

## Absolute rules (Don'ts)

This list is the single canonical source. `.claude/rules/forbidden.md` carries a resident subset and `.claude/rules/design.md` points here. Each item ends with a `dont:` key matched by slug in `tests/contract/agent-instructions/design-donts-parity.contract.test.ts`: rewrite the sentence freely, never the key.

- Clicking a topology node opens a fullscreen or full-bleed detail modal. The default is ego focus plus a compact panel docked at the map's right inset; full detail is opt-in from inside that panel. <!--dont:node-click-fullscreen-modal-->
- Floating-box soup: unrelated panels, popovers, HUD, minimap and cards at equal visual weight. <!--dont:floating-box-soup-->
- Stacked popovers, or a popover over a modal, without closing or dimming the previous surface. <!--dont:stacked-popover-->
- A blocking composer or modal without a dim, scrim or suppressed parent interaction. <!--dont:modal-without-dim-->
- A one-off topology `clamp(...)`, shadow, radius, z-order, easing or duration without a `--topology-*` token and verifier marker. <!--dont:oneoff-topology-value-->
- Overlap tolerated because the surface "mostly still works": the overlap count is zero for fixed and card surfaces in the tested state. <!--dont:overlap-tolerated-->
- Decorative arrows trailing a label (`Open →`, `Details →`, a trailing `ArrowRight` or `ArrowUpRight`). <!--dont:decorative-trailing-arrow-->
- Cards in one grid whose height is decided by how much text each contains. <!--dont:content-decided-card-height-->

Also held, with no resident key: tokenless visual values in JSX; touch-density chrome (48px and up) on desktop; a light panel slab on a dark canvas; `--map-*` referenced from a page outside topology; reviving the app-embedded terminal dock (`--terminal-*`, `docs/AGENT-GRAPH-WORKFLOW.md`); rarity glow, gems, loot and particles on decision surfaces. Every Don't holds app-wide.

### Arrows carry information or they don't ship

A trailing arrow after a link label adds nothing: the label already says where you go. Test: remove the arrow and read the label aloud; if nothing was lost, it was decoration. An arrow is allowed when it is the data: a path `{source} → {target}`, an ordering, a menu path `Settings → Developer`, cause and effect, a leading `↗` on a link that leaves the app, a chevron as disclosure state. `label-decoration` scans `messages/*.json` for a trailing arrow, a bare `↗` in JSX without `data-external-link-marker`, and a lone arrow element at the end of a label, matched by position (its next non-whitespace sibling is the parent's closing tag), never by glyph.

### Dimensional regularity

A container's dimensions are a design decision, not a byproduct of its contents. In a repeating set: fix the anatomy (the same named slots in the same order; an absent slot is worse than an empty one); reserve the space of an optional clause; truncate list slots to a fixed count with a remainder caption, never "however many fit"; equalize rows as well as columns when the set reads as one field; clamp long titles with the full value on hover, focus or in the detail. A one-off card sizes to its content.

### One text edge per column

Bare text, labels, headings, table labels and row text in one column start on one x. Backgrounds, borders and hover fills may extend past that edge (`-mx-*` paired with the matching `px-*`); text never steps in. A boxed control keeps its inner padding, so its box sits on the edge; cards in a list sit exactly on the column's box edge. A full-width `shape: "row"` emits `w-full`, so a bare `-mx-*` bleeds left only: split the column's inset between the list and the row.

**Control groups fill their column.** A row of controls that would wrap becomes a grid that fills the width: `grid grid-cols-2`, `w-full` on every control, `col-span-2` on the odd child, and a control whose label is a sentence takes the whole row. Inline rows that never wrap at their design width are left alone.

## System growth rules

How to extend the system, in order.

0. **Look for the existing value first**: the role's token (`pnpm design:tokens -- --prefix=<role>`), the ramp rules here, and `git log --oneline -S'<token>' -- app/globals.css app/styles` for why it is what it is. A value created without looking is a second system.
1. **Look at slots before adding axes.** Axes multiply (three axes, eight combinations); components add. If only the shell changes, an axis; if text, icon or badge changes, a part.
2. **Do not make two tags with the same appearance** (a byte-identical `<button>` and `<Link>`). One component replaces its underlying element; `asChild` is an escape hatch only where a duplicate would otherwise appear, and its child must spread all props, accept a ref and stay accessible, pinned by contract tests, or one side is removed.
3. **To change the specification, prove it on every screen.** A token, ramp, primitive or design rule change takes `pnpm ui:look` over every route in `tests/e2e/audited-routes.ts` and the design-system audit (`/ui-proof`); a changed gate also takes `/gate-probe`.
4. **Count how many are blocked.** A new shape, tone or axis comes with the number of controls it would leave outside the system; uncounted, it is a preference.
5. **A rule and its gate land in the same pull request.** Count violations before enabling a gate (`/gate-probe`) and plant a flaw that turns it red.
6. **Changing a library is a last resort**: a new dependency needs its reason in the pull request, and replacing `cva` needs evidence that slots cannot be done with it.
