# Product Catalog

This area owns one question: what does the shopper get to see? What this t-shirt costs today, which colours and sizes exist, which category it lives in, and what earlier buyers said about it. Everything a shopper sees before deciding to buy is decided here.

The work starts on the operator's side: register the product, place it in a category, attach options like colour and size, set the price. Then the shopper's half follows: they find the product through search or the category tree, meet it on the detail page, and next to it sit related-product recommendations and reviews.

The most frequent argument here is "is this the same product?". A t-shirt in another colour is either an option of one product or a separate product, and the answer changes stock counts, reviews and search results alike. This store's rule: if it is sold and counted separately, it is a separate product.

The boundary runs like this: this area says what a product is, the inventory area says how many are left, and marketing says why to buy it now. One thing to watch: a price belongs not to a product but to a product at a moment in time, because the order area needs what yesterday's buyer actually paid to calculate a refund correctly.
